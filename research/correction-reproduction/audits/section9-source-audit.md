# Section 9 Source Audit: Claims in the Manuscript Against the Two Books

Sources examined. End Times Fascism (Klein and Taylor) as a PDF that is a pre-publication proof, with placeholders in its front matter. Page numbers below are PDF page indexes, not printed pages, and wording must be rechecked against the published edition. The Nerd Reich (Durán) as an EPUB with no printed pagination in the text. Chapter and endnote numbers are reliable, and the page numbers come from the book's own index, so they are approximate.

Findings use three labels. Supported means the passage says what the manuscript says. Supported with qualification means it says most of it but the manuscript's wording needs adjusting. Not established means the material examined does not contain the claim.

Type column. Interpretation means the authors' own reading. Reported event means a documented event relayed from journalism or records. Quoted third party means the authors quote someone else's analysis.

## Additional primary check completed

The Army's own release of July 31, 2025 was read directly. It states that the Army awarded Palantir an enterprise agreement with a performance period of up to ten years, that it merges 75 contracts (15 prime and 60 related) into one, that the $10 billion figure is a ceiling and not an obligation, that the consolidation removes contract and reseller pass-through fees, that it includes volume-based discounts, and that the Army remains committed to driving robust competition. The release does not state a reassessment schedule. Trade reporting adds that a solicitation provides for reassessment every 18 to 24 months, that the Army's minimum guarantee was not disclosed, and that an Army contracting official said the Army is considering ten to fifteen further enterprise agreements for other commercial software. Those three points come from secondary reports and are to be confirmed in the solicitation and from the Army. A contract number appears in a secondary aggregator and should be confirmed against the Defense Department contract announcement.

## Findings that require changes to the manuscript

The manuscript says the AI firms have "differing stated aims" as though the book reported it. That is the essay's own characterization and is not established, so the text should present it as such. The manuscript should also say that both books rely on the same analysis of the authoritarian stack, so they are not independent confirmations. On the consolidation, the book's text speaks of announced plans, but the Army's own release shows the agreement was awarded, so the manuscript now cites that release and keeps the distinction between a ceiling and obligated funds. The phrase "relationships that persist across administrations" should be narrowed to the continuity of Palantir's government contracting.

## What this audit does not establish

It checks what the books say, not whether it is true. Every Durán item is a press report relayed second hand, and the next step for each is the underlying article and then the primary record: Army and Defense Department announcements, contract documents, and federal award and obligation data. None of the items shows that any consolidation raised dependency, which needs evidence of switching costs and contestability.

## Federal award data check (October 31, 2026)

Palantir's obligations were pulled from USAspending.gov and are described in palantir-award-data-notes.md, with the tables in three CSV files. D1 is reconciled as above. D6 is now supported for continuity and growth of obligations across administrations (about $52 million a year under the Obama administration, $179 million under the first Trump administration, $419 million under the Biden administration, and about $1.7 billion annualized so far under the second), though continuity of obligations is not persistence of dependency. D3c: the agreement exists in the federal record, signed July 31, 2025, with a $10 billion ceiling, no obligations on the vehicle itself, a not-competed only-one-source coding, and 40 orders totaling $1.23 billion by October 31, 2026. D2 and D4 to D5 were not checked against federal data.
