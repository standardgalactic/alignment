# Reference Audit Sheet: When Correction Becomes Reproduction

Three different checks are needed, and passing one does not imply the others. Bibliographic verification confirms that a work exists and its details are correct. Quotation and interpretation verification confirms that the manuscript represents what the work says, with the supporting passage recorded. Causal-evidence verification confirms that a record supports the inference drawn from it, so that an award shows an award and does not by itself show that dependency rose.

Status key. Confirmed means checked against the source or a publisher record in this project, for the bibliographic details only. Memory means written from memory and still to be checked against the original.

## Interpretive checks completed against the full texts

Merton (1936), pp. 894-904, read from the supplied scan. Merton lists four factors: ignorance, error, the imperious immediacy of interest, and basic values (the realization of values leading to their renunciation, illustrated by Weber on the Protestant ethic). He then discusses, as a separate circumstance peculiar to human conduct, that public predictions become part of the situation and tend to change its course (Marx's prediction as the example, Venn's suicidal prophecies cited). The earlier manuscript list omitted basic values and presented the self-defeating prediction as one of the factors. Corrected in Section 3 to match the article.

Riordan (2021), read in full text. Confirmed: the sentence 'although what he postulated is speculative, it is nevertheless a hypothesis concerning human evolution' appears in the introduction, so the short quotation is accurate. Confirmed: the abstract and conclusion state commonalities with Wrangham's execution hypothesis, Dunbar's storytelling hypothesis and DeScioli and Kurzban's non-consequentialist morality hypothesis. Confirmed: the text notes little direct empirical evidence for the premise that dominance hierarchies pacify other animals, and the conclusion allows that Girard's assumption about rarity of lethal conspecific violence may be inaccurate. The conclusion also calls the scapegoat addition arguably parsimonious, which the manuscript does not mention and which a fair reading of Riordan's overall stance should keep in view.
